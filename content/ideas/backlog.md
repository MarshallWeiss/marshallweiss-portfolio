### The Golden Age of Web Design
- **Added**: 2026-02-22
- **Category**: Ideas, Articles
- **Status**: captured
- **Notes**: We might be living in the golden age of web design right now. Tools like Framer, AI-assisted coding, and modern frameworks let us create websites faster and more easily than ever — but AI hasn't yet degraded the craft. There's a window where the tools are empowering without being destructive. At some point AI-generated sites will flood the market, homogenize aesthetics further, and devalue the work. But right now, skilled designers can leverage these tools to produce great work at unprecedented speed. Worth capturing this moment before it passes.

### The Privacy Amnesia: How AI Made Us Forget We Ever Cared

- **Added**: 2026-02-14
- **Category**: Ideas, Articles
- **Status**: captured
- **Notes**: Users are massively, completely forgetting about privacy in their AI experiences. This isn't new—it's the continuation of a decades-long pattern where tech companies progressively take more and more of your privacy. You become accustomed to it incrementally. First it was cookies, then location tracking, then always-on microphones, now it's feeding your entire life into AI models. Each step feels small, but the cumulative effect is staggering. How did we go from "I don't want websites tracking me" to "here's my entire email history, analyze it and tell me what to do"? The boiling frog phenomenon of privacy erosion, now accelerated by AI's usefulness.

### The Copyright Paradox: When Every Web Product Looks the Same, What's Left to Protect?

- **Added**: 2026-02-14
- **Category**: Ideas, Articles
- **Status**: captured
- **Notes**: What is copyright when it comes to web products and software design? Everyone's copying everyone now—there's this crazy coalescence into one universal style. From a design perspective, it's fascinating and bizarre: the total homogenization of web aesthetics. But legally, what can you even copyright? If all SaaS products look identical (same layouts, same interactions, same "startup aesthetic"), where's the line between inspiration and infringement? When copying becomes the norm, does copyright still mean anything? Explores both the design phenomenon (why everything looks the same) and the legal question (what's protectable when everyone's doing it).

### The Self-Cannibalizing Software Economy: When AI Makes Every Feature Instantly Replicable

- **Added**: 2026-02-14
- **Status**: captured
- **Notes**: With AI, there comes a point where software design outpaces itself. Every new tool created, every new idea immediately becomes reabsorbed by AI and its users. Users can just ask the AI to replicate that same feature natively—like creating a personal version of Whispr Flow when running out of free weekly words. What does this mean for software design, for SaaS businesses? Will they die? What will that mean for software creation in general? Every person for themselves? Whoever can make software autonomously with AI? This makes workflow optimization, process design, and focus some of the most important skills in product design.

Upload Atlantic articles, train on their patterns, output new work that feels identical but legally distinct. Copyright law assumes copying requires effort and leaves obvious traces—but what happens when you can absorb and remix someone's life's work in an afternoon? The law is designed for photocopiers in a world of neural networks. Not just "copyright is outdated" but: what does ownership even mean when style, voice, and approach—the actual value—can be extracted and redeployed without technically copying a single sentence? And if we can't protect that, what exactly are we protecting?
