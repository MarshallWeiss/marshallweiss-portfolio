In the winter of 1921, a Soviet engineer named Leonid Krasin stood before the Presidium and declared that science would soon achieve "the resurrection of the dead." The Bolsheviks took notes. Within a decade, they'd built an entire apparatus of central planning justified by the promise that technology plus ideology would transcend human limitation itself—create the New Soviet Man, conquer mortality, usher in paradise through sufficient application of scientific rationality.

[Sam Altman said in January 2025:](https://time.com/7205596/sam-altman-superintelligence-agi/) "We are now confident we know how to build AGI as we have traditionally understood it. We are beginning to turn our aim beyond that, to superintelligence in the true sense of the word. With superintelligence, we can do anything else."

The structure is identical: external force (technology) plus proper implementation equals transcendence of human limits. The century changes. The terminology evolves. The messianism doesn't. And catastrophic failure follows with such regularity that we should perhaps consider it a feature, not a bug, of asking material systems to deliver spiritual salvation.

## The Template

[Émile Torres spent years documenting](https://www.cbc.ca/radio/ideas/tech-billionaires-ai-utopia-1.7440698) what he calls the TESCREAL framework—Transhumanism, Extropianism, Singularitarianism, Cosmism, Rationalism, Effective Altruism, Longtermism. These apparently separate ideologies are interconnected descendants of twentieth-century eugenics movements, he argues. "The tech billionaires' promise of techno-utopia is dangerous because it follows the exact template of previous movements that ended in authoritarianism or collapse."

The template has remarkable consistency. First, identify a transformative technology that promises to overcome fundamental human constraints. Factories will end scarcity. Telephones will unify humanity. Computers will democratize knowledge. [Demis Hassabis describes](https://kantrowitz.medium.com/google-deepmind-ceo-demis-hassabis-the-path-to-agi-llm-creativity-and-google-smart-glasses-d09ddcd471a2) Google DeepMind's mission as "solving intelligence and then using it to solve everything else."

Second, frame resistance as moral failure or dangerous ignorance. [Peter Thiel argues that](https://www.techpolicy.press/digital-eugenics-and-the-extinction-of-humanity/) "fearing or regulating artificial intelligence or other technologies, or opposing technological progress, would hasten the coming of the Antichrist." [Marc Andreessen goes further:](https://a16z.com/the-techno-optimist-manifesto/) "Because AI might be used to save lives, any pause on AI development that limits preventable deaths is a form of murder." Questioning becomes heresy. Caution becomes complicity in death.

Third, concentrate resources and power in the hands of those building the transformation, justified by the stakes and urgency. [OpenAI raised $40 billion](https://techfundingnews.com/openai-anthropic-xai-ai-funding-trends-2025/) and is valued at $300 billion. [Anthropic is valued at $183 billion.](https://techfundingnews.com/openai-anthropic-xai-ai-funding-trends-2025/) Five companies [spent $197 billion on AI infrastructure](https://techfundingnews.com/openai-anthropic-xai-ai-funding-trends-2025/) in 2024 alone. [The tech industry spent $61 billion on lobbying,](https://ainowinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/FINAL-20250609_AINowLandscapeReport_Full.pdf) with Elon Musk [personally contributing $288 million](https://ainowinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/FINAL-20250609_AINowLandscapeReport_Full.pdf) in campaign funds. Only we understand what's coming. Only we can be trusted with this power. Democratic deliberation is too slow.

Fourth, promise that breakthrough is imminent. Always imminent. Musk said AGI by 2025, then 2026. Altman [declared AGI "essentially solved"](https://time.com/7205596/sam-altman-superintelligence-agi/) while simultaneously saying years remain. [Dario Amodei says](https://tamiltech.in/public/article/anthropic-ceo-dario-amodei-davos-2026-interview-agi-predictions) he's "more confident than I've ever been" despite no fundamental breakthrough justifying that confidence. [As one policy analysis noted,](https://casi.stanford.edu/news/power-truth-ai-narratives-public-trust-and-new-tech-empire) "It is strategic for Sam Altman to publicly hope-dict AGI by 2025 so as to reinforce narrative of AI as transformative and, soon, inevitable—a narrative investors often find irresistible."

Fifth, when it fails, the infrastructure of power remains. Soviet cybernetics was quietly shelved by the 1970s. The central planning apparatus it justified continued. Nineteenth-century utopian communes collapsed. Capital concentration didn't.

We are currently somewhere between stages three and four.

## The Same Script, Different Century

The Soviets weren't inventing a new pattern—they were inheriting one. [Asif Siddiqi describes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_utopianism) Soviet technological utopianism as a "millenarian mantra from inception." The promise was always the same: science would transform humanity, free us from constraints that had limited our species for millennia, create abundance without labor. What arrived instead was central planning by technocrats who claimed special knowledge, concentration of power in hands of those promising imminent breakthrough, and spectacular failure obscured by propaganda about how close we were to paradise.

But the Soviets had teachers. Nineteenth-century industrial utopianism promised that factories would liberate workers from toil and deliver an age of leisure and abundance. What emerged was child labor so severe that Massachusetts [created the Bureau of Labor Statistics specifically to count](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_utopianism) "children's arms torn off" in machinery. The abundance arrived. It concentrated in different hands than the ones that did the laboring.

[Early twentieth-century Italian Futurists,](https://www.fastcompany.com/91400946/musks-techno-utopianism-20th-century-europe) inspired by telephones and automobiles, envisioned a "New Human" transformed by speed and technology. They promised to "destroy the old world to make way for the new." The cult of the machine, the promise of the New Human, the rhetoric of creative destruction—these didn't stay metaphorical. They became literal programs of human elimination in service of technological perfection.

[Ray Kurzweil predicts](https://bernardmarr.com/28-best-quotes-about-artificial-intelligence/) "by 2045, we will have multiplied the intelligence, the human biological machine intelligence of our civilization a billion-fold." Multiplied beyond recognition until humanity becomes something that isn't human anymore. Sundar Pichai [calls AI](https://bernardmarr.com/28-best-quotes-about-artificial-intelligence/) "probably the most important thing humanity has ever worked on. I think of it as something more profound than electricity or fire." Fire is literally the Promethean gift from gods to humans in mythology—divine technology that separates us from animals.

[Arthur Mensch, CEO of Mistral AI,](https://fortune.com/europe/article/mistral-boss-tech-ceos-obsession-ai-outsmarting-humans-very-religious-fascination/) is one of the few insiders willing to name what he's watching: "The whole AGI rhetoric is about creating God." Even [Mark Zuckerberg observed](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/silicon-valley-using-religious-language-talk-ai-rcna227987) that "when people in the tech industry talk about building this one true AI, it's almost as if they think they're creating God or something."

When insiders are using theological language to describe what their colleagues are doing, we might want to take seriously that this is a religious movement, not a technical one. And religious movements promising material salvation through technological breakthrough have a clear historical record.

## Why the Pattern Keeps Repeating

If the pattern is this consistent, this documented, this catastrophic in its outcomes—why do we keep falling for it?

Humans need meaning, purpose, transcendence, connection to something larger than themselves. These aren't luxuries. When traditional sources of these goods collapse, the need doesn't disappear. It gets redirected.

[Greg Epstein, Humanist Chaplain at Harvard and MIT,](https://www.modernreformation.org/resources/essays/ai-and-silicon-valleys-spirituality-without-religion) counsels the actual people building AGI. What he witnesses is ["technology has become our new faith."](https://www.modernreformation.org/resources/essays/ai-and-silicon-valleys-spirituality-without-religion) The Bay Area shows some of the nation's steepest declines in religious affiliation over the past two decades. It also has, [according to venture capitalist Somesh Dash,](https://fortune.com/2025/09/18/ivp-somesh-dash-venture-capital-spirituality-peter-thiel-agi/) an "epidemic of loneliness" and a "meaning crisis."

These aren't separate phenomena. In the absence of traditional structures for meaning—religion, civic participation, stable communities—technology has filled the void as a source of ultimate answers rather than a set of tools.

John Calvin, sixteenth-century Reformed theologian, [had a phrase:](https://www.modernreformation.org/resources/essays/ai-and-silicon-valleys-spirituality-without-religion) "Man's nature, so to speak, is a perpetual factory of idols." He meant that humans can't not worship—we can only choose what we worship. When we're unmoored from traditions that encode wisdom about human limits, we don't become rational actors. We build new gods. We just call them by different names.

This explains why the pattern repeats despite its consistent failure. Each generation experiences the same existential needs, encounters the same collapse of meaning-making structures, and reaches for technological transcendence because we've systematically dismantled every other framework for understanding limitation, mortality, and finitude.

The Soviets had demolished religious tradition in favor of scientific materialism. When that left a vacuum, technological utopianism filled it. Early twentieth-century Futurists were reacting to the collapse of old European orders and the meaninglessness of industrial modernity. When that left a vacuum, they reached for the cult of the machine. Nineteenth-century industrialists operated in a world where Enlightenment rationalism had weakened religious authority but offered no replacement structure for meaning. When that left a vacuum, they promised that factories would deliver what churches could not.

Silicon Valley has the lowest religious affiliation rates in America, has dissolved community bonds in favor of individual optimization, has replaced ritual with productivity metrics. And now it promises that sufficient computation will solve the God-shaped hole.

Calvin understood what Silicon Valley has forgotten: the hole is shaped that way for a reason. You can't fill a need for human connection with computation. You can't solve finitude with scale. You can't answer existential questions with engineering solutions. What happens when you try anyway? You build an idol. And idolatry, historically, has never ended with enlightenment. It ends with disillusionment and the consolidation of power in the hands of the priests who claimed to speak for the god that never arrived.

## The Function of Both Narratives

Silicon Valley has learned to weaponize both salvation and apocalypse simultaneously.

[Timnit Gebru, founder of the Distributed AI Research Institute,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TESCREAL) identified the pattern: Both utopian ("boomer") and dystopian ("doomer") narratives about AGI serve identical functions. "They use hypothetical AI-driven apocalypses and utopian futures to justify unlimited research, development, and deregulation of technology."

Whether the story is salvation or catastrophe, the conclusion is the same: vast resources and power must be concentrated in the hands of those building AGI. [As one analysis noted,](https://medium.com/ipg-media-lab/the-battle-is-on-for-control-over-the-ai-narrative-fd617583c596) "both 'Boomer' and 'Doomer' narratives paint the technology as supremely powerful and therefore one that must be controlled very tightly by a small group of people. It just so happens, the people that should be controlling the technology are the people espousing those narratives."

The brilliance is that you can switch between narratives depending on the audience. To investors: utopia, abundance, ["we can do anything else."](https://time.com/7205596/sam-altman-superintelligence-agi/) To regulators: existential risk, dangerous if mishandled, only we can be trusted. Both stories consolidate power in the same hands. Both justify present-tense harm by fixating attention on hypothetical future transformation.

The present-tense harms are substantial. In 2025, [approximately 55,000 workers were laid off](https://fortune.com/2026/02/10/ai-taking-jobs-report-tristan-harris-google-ethicist-agi-technology/) in AI-related workforce reorganizations. [Stanford research showed](https://fortune.com/2026/02/10/ai-taking-jobs-report-tristan-harris-google-ethicist-agi-technology/) a 13% decline in jobs for early-career workers since late 2022. In Kenya and Venezuela, content moderators are paid $1-2 per hour to review traumatic material for AI training datasets.

[Gebru argues that](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TESCREAL) "by considering only far-reaching future consequences, creating hype for unproven technology, and fear-mongering, TESCREALists distract from the impacts of technology that may adversely affect society, disproportionately harm minorities through algorithmic bias, and have a detrimental impact on the environment."

This is the same function Soviet technological utopianism served. Focus on the imminent paradise or catastrophe, and you don't have to account for the bodies in the present. The promise of future transformation justifies current exploitation. The urgency of the breakthrough justifies the concentration of power.

Every previous iteration of this pattern followed the same playbook. What's being obscured now is identical to what was obscured then: Who benefits from the narrative regardless of whether the promised transformation arrives? Who consolidates power while we're fixated on the messianic timeline? What democratic choice is being foreclosed while we're told the trajectory is inevitable?

## The Historical Record

Every previous technological messianism has ended in violence, authoritarianism, quiet abandonment, or disillusionment and backlash—often some combination of all four.

Early twentieth-century Futurism delivered fascism. Soviet technological utopianism justified central planning by technical experts and concentration of power in hands of those who claimed special knowledge about the inevitable trajectory of history. The technology failed. The power structure remained. Most technological messianisms don't explode—they just slowly deflate as the timeline keeps shifting, the promised transformation keeps not arriving, and people move on. The infrastructure built to deliver paradise gets repurposed for more mundane exploitation. The ideological justification quietly disappears but the concentration of capital and power persists.

Sometimes the gap between promise and reality becomes so stark that the entire framework collapses in public consciousness, usually with violent political consequences as people who were promised transcendence get industrial-scale disappointment instead.

The pattern has played out enough times that we have good data on outcomes. None of them are "and then the promised transformation actually arrived and everything was fine."

The question isn't whether AGI will work as advertised. The question is what happens to the power structures being built right now while we wait to find out. Because history suggests that by the time we discover whether the god arrives, the priests will have already secured control of the temple, and getting them out will be considerably harder than letting them in.

## What Gets Foreclosed

The AGI inevitability narrative doesn't just promise a specific future—it makes alternative futures seem impossible.

[Vitalik Buterin has outlined](https://bitcoinist.com/vitalik-buterin-ethereum-ai-vision-as-alternative/) an approach prioritizing "decentralization, privacy, and verification over scale and speed"—AI designed to augment human agency rather than replace it, governed democratically rather than by corporate boards. [The Cooperative AI Foundation](https://www.cooperativeai.com/foundation) researches cooperative intelligence rather than competitive superintelligence. [Community-centered AI practices are emerging,](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14626268.2025.2519125) particularly from the Global South, assuming technology should serve existing communities rather than remake them.

These aren't naive or unworkable approaches. But they're treated as irrelevant because the dominant narrative frames AGI as inevitable. And when one vision seems inevitable, whoever controls that vision controls everything. That foreclosure of imagination is itself a power play.

The Soviets didn't just build their technocratic apparatus—they made alternative visions seem like dangerous romanticism or capitalist propaganda. The Futurists didn't just promise the New Human—they framed anyone who questioned the trajectory as obstacles to be destroyed. The pattern includes not just the messianic promise but the elimination of alternatives.

[As one analysis notes,](https://www.codastory.com/authoritarian-tech/captured-silicon-valley-future-religion-artificial-intelligence/) "Silicon Valley is building a future we never chose" and "proceeding as if they had a mandate on behalf of everyone." A small group of Effective Altruists and longtermists now [dominates how the White House, Congress, and think tanks approach AI policy,](https://venturebeat.com/ai/ai-and-policy-leaders-debate-web-of-effective-altruism-in-ai-security-the-ai-beat) despite representing a tiny fraction of the tech community or broader public. [Yoshua Bengio observes](https://yoshuabengio.org/2024/10/30/implications-of-artificial-general-intelligence-on-national-and-international-security/) that "control of these technologies is extraordinarily concentrated" and "superior intelligence from AGI could provide unequaled strategic advantages on a global scale and tip the balance in favor of a few (companies, countries or individuals), while causing great harm to many others."

The question we should be asking isn't "Will AGI arrive?" but "Why is only one vision of AI's future being treated as viable?" When slow, democratic, community-centered, cooperative approaches are dismissed as naive while the rush toward god-building is treated as inevitable—that should raise questions about whose interests are served by the inevitability narrative itself.

## Pattern Recognition

We've built this god before. We know how it ends. The century changes, the technology shifts, the terminology evolves—but the structure remains remarkably consistent. And that consistency means we don't have to wait for the outcome to know where this leads.

Every time humans have tried to build technological salvation, we've ended up building tyranny instead—not because we were stupid, but because asking material systems to deliver spiritual goods is a category error that leads, with remarkable consistency, to the same place: a small group claiming special knowledge about humanity's ultimate destiny, accumulating extraordinary power and resources in service of an imminent breakthrough that keeps not arriving, while everyone else is expected to trust them and accept present-tense harm as necessary price of future transformation.

The resistance available to us is refusing to believe that this time is different. Refusing to accept that the pattern won't repeat just because the technology is digital instead of mechanical. Refusing the narrative that democratic deliberation is too slow, that questions are heresy, that the trajectory is inevitable.

[Religious communities have noticed what secular rationalists have missed.](https://aiandfaith.org/insights/enchanted-by-ai/) In December 2025, the Future of Life Institute brought together Christian, Muslim, Jewish, and Hindu leaders specifically to discuss AI theologically. The Vatican [warned](https://aiandfaith.org/insights/enchanted-by-ai/) that "we're in danger of worshiping AI as God." [Nigerian church leaders](https://aiandfaith.org/insights/enchanted-by-ai/) convened to address the "emerging impacts and threats of the AGI race."

These communities recognize something Silicon Valley has forgotten: wisdom traditions exist specifically because humans keep making the same category errors. We keep trying to solve finitude with scale, connection with computation, meaning with engineering. We keep building idols. And the traditions that survived are the ones that developed frameworks for recognizing and resisting that impulse.

[As these religious leaders noted:](https://aiandfaith.org/insights/enchanted-by-ai/) "Prayers formed in uncertainty and pain, and sermons born from wrestling with sacred texts, are spiritual disciplines." The wrestling is the point. The limitation is the terrain on which meaning gets made. Trying to bypass struggle through technological transcendence doesn't deliver meaning—it abandons the only process through which meaning can emerge.

The choice available to us is the same choice that's always been available: recognize the pattern, refuse the messianism, insist on democratic deliberation, demand accountability for present harm rather than accepting it as price of hypothetical future transformation, and remember that when someone promises to solve "everything else," they're asking for power over everything else.

The god won't arrive. The priests will remain. And by the time we figure that out, getting them out of the temple they've built while we waited will be considerably harder than not letting them in.

History doesn't repeat. But it rhymes. And the rhyme scheme of technological messianism is remarkably consistent. Maybe pattern recognition is enough. Maybe it has to be.
